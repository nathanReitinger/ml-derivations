Small enough to reproduce what it never saw.

It trains, right here in your browser, on 50 tiny ascii drawings — 20×10 characters, only two symbols. The rest of the pack is held out and never shown to it. Training doesn't stop on a step count; it stops once a quality gate confirms the model is actually good at this, checked only against the training set. Only then does it start generating, one drawing at a time, each one scored by SSIM against the held-out set. Generation stops the instant one lands close enough to a drawing the model never trained on.

That's the point being demonstrated: shrink the problem space enough — a small grid, two symbols, repetitive parametric shapes (boxes, rings, stripes) — and a generative model's outputs can land on data it was never trained on, purely because there are only so many ways to draw a ring of a given radius. It's a brute-force search either way, hashcat-style: same held-out target set the whole time, you just keep sampling until one attempt lands close enough. The threshold below controls how close "close" has to be.
